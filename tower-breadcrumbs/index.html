Back to a place you actually recorded.

“Last reception” saves where your phone reported cellular service. “Last data” separately records where Android validated a cellular internet connection. Select either point to see a true-north bearing and distance from your current GPS position.

Read the landscape. Keep the details close.

A north-up map, clear coordinates and a compact return panel keep the important information together. The logbook holds your journeys and lets you export a selected trail as CSV.

Development build shown with a simulated trail. The background is a coordinate grid, not a downloaded road map.

Bring your maps and cell data.

Import a regional raster MBTiles map before you leave. The coordinate grid and saved trail work without internet; roads appear where your imported map has tiles. This first version has no built-in area downloader or worldwide tower database.

Import estimated cell locations from a compatible CSV. Carrier names such as T-Mobile or Verizon and radio labels such as 5G/NR, 4G/LTE and 3G appear when supplied by the dataset. Unknown fields stay unknown. Imported positions may be estimated cell centers rather than physical towers.

Designed for the field, with honest limits.

GPS needs a usable satellite view. The bearing is a straight line, not a safe walking route. Terrain, carrier compatibility, outages and network changes can all affect reception. A saved point or nearby cell does not guarantee service. Keep an independent navigation and emergency plan.

The planned app is free with ads and a one-time $0.99 US purchase to remove all ads. Store availability and local pricing will be confirmed at launch. Full-screen ads are excluded from active GPS navigation.
