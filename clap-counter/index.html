Clap Counter · Android · In development

Make a sound.
See it count.

A playful way to count claps, watch sound levels, and keep track of your sessions. A bright live trace turns microphone activity into something you can see.

How it worksHelp & support

Development preview for Android 8 and newer. A public Google Play download is not available here yet.

Watch the sound

A scrolling green trace, digital decibel readout, and threshold line make microphone activity visible.

Count your way

Choose claps, broader sound events, or manual taps. Adjust sensitivity or calibrate for the room.

Keep the result

Pause without adding time. Save, rename, delete, or share a text summary from your local history.

A few taps to get started

Start. Clap. Save.

  1. Choose your counting mode in Settings. Sound modes ask for microphone permission; Manual does not.
  2. Tap Start and watch the counter and graph. Pause whenever you need a break.
  3. Tap Stop & save to keep the result in History. Counting pauses when you leave the app.

Your sounds stay with you.

The app processes microphone samples in memory. It does not save raw audio recordings or upload microphone samples. Ads and Google Play services have their own data practices, explained in the privacy policy.

Free counting. A simple ad-removal option.

The planned release is free with occasional interstitial ads after completed sessions. A one-time US$0.99 Remove Ads purchase is planned, with local prices supplied by Google Play. No subscription. Store setup and purchase testing are still in progress.

A useful counter, with honest limits.

The display reports dBFS, a digital microphone signal level—not calibrated room-noise decibels. This is not a medical monitor or sound-safety meter. Some non-clap sounds can count: testing found that a sudden steady tone can produce one false count. Real-room microphone testing is ongoing.
